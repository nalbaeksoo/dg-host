## Evidence

- Read-only preflight passed immediately before the attempt: one verified RTL9210B 1.33.98 BIN, no CFG, valid MPTool signature/hash, and exact target match.
- Before attempt session ID: `833530967574`.

  `endpoint 0x00: status 0xe00002ed (transaction error)`.
- macOS then logged loss of the device connection and termination of `IOUSBMassStorageDriver`.
- The same device re-enumerated about 3.36 seconds later at 480 Mbps.
- After attempt session ID: `911974247531`.
- After MPTool closed, the target was active with `busy 0`, and `IOUSBMassStorageDriver` plus the Realtek RTL9210 SCSI logical unit were attached again.
- Kernel logging continued to report repeated `endpoint 0x81: status 0xe0005000 (pipe stalled)` errors.

## Assessment

The device is not bricked and is operating on the prior `1.32.50` firmware. The `0%` failure, unchanged firmware version, and successful re-enumeration on the old firmware show that no firmware-update progress was confirmed and that 1.33.98 did not complete. A `0%` UI does not prove that absolutely no flash byte was attempted or changed, so this record must not be treated as proof of a completely untouched flash region; there is no positive evidence of a partial 1.33.98 installation either.

The disconnect and re-enumeration can be part of MPTool's transition into firmware-update mode, so the kernel events alone do not prove that the cable or hub physically dropped. However, the device returned in normal `f001` mode and MPTool then declared failure, while the pre-existing mass-storage stalls immediately resumed. MPTool's detailed `FwUpdate fail` reason is emitted to standard output and was not persisted by this GUI launch, so the exact first internal failure cannot be recovered from this attempt.

Do not retry automatically through this same 480 Mbps macOS path. A future attempt should use a stable direct USB port on a Windows machine, with no intervening hub where possible, the SSD slot empty, stable external power, the exact verified BIN, and no CFG. If another macOS attempt is considered, MPTool should first be launched with standard output captured so its exact internal failure reason is retained. A new explicit approval is required before another flash attempt.

In the same dock topology, the upstream USB 3.1 hub is at 10 Gbps and the card-reader/LAN branches are at 5 Gbps, so the whole dock connection is not falling back to USB 2.0; the limitation is specific to the RTL9210 branch in its current empty-slot state.

Conclusion: the power cycle was successful and the bridge survived, but it returned to the same pre-update behavior. The repeatable 8 Hz stall pattern and `MediumType=None` support an empty-LUN polling/firmware behavior rather than a transient stale USB session.
